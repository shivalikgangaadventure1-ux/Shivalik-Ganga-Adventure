Every rafting package lists a "grade," but what does Grade II or Grade IV actually mean for your trip? Rapid grades follow the International Scale of River Difficulty, a standard used by rafting operators worldwide, running from Grade I (easiest) to Grade VI (essentially unraftable commercially). It measures wave size, obstacle density, how much active paddling and route-reading a rapid demands, and how much margin for error you have if something goes wrong. Here's what each tier we run actually feels like on the water, not just the textbook definition.

## Grade I–II: Easy to Moderate

Small waves, a few riffles, and mostly clear passages between them. Grade I is close to flat water; Grade II adds gentle, regular waves you can see coming and steer around well in advance. This is where our [Brahmpuri to Rishikesh package](/packages/brahmpuri-to-rishikesh) sits, a gentle, forgiving introduction that's ideal for first-timers, families, and children above the minimum age. You'll get splashed, you'll paddle as a team, but there's no real risk of the raft flipping and long calm stretches to relax between rapids.

## Grade III: Moderate to Difficult

Irregular waves, some larger drops, and rapids that require active paddling and teamwork to navigate cleanly. You need to follow your guide's commands promptly, not just paddle when you feel like it. Most of our core routes, including [Shivpuri to Rishikesh](/packages/shivpuri-to-rishikesh) and [Marine Drive to Rishikesh](/packages/marine-drive-to-rishikesh), fall in this range, taking in named rapids like Roller Coaster and Golf Course. Expect to get properly soaked, occasionally lose your footing inside the raft, and need genuine paddling effort through the rapid sequences. Reasonable fitness helps, but you don't need prior rafting experience.

## Grade III+ to IV: Difficult to Very Difficult

Powerful, continuous rapids with larger drops, more obstacles, and less margin for error. The gaps between rapids are shorter and the current is stronger throughout. Routes like [Kaudiyala to Rishikesh](/packages/kaudiyala-to-rishikesh) and our most intense run, [Kaudiyala to Shivpuri Extreme](/packages/kaudiyala-to-shivpuri-extreme), sit here, covering named rapids including Sweet Sixteen and Terminator. These routes are best suited to rafters with some prior experience or strong water confidence, run with senior crew and an extended safety briefing, and use smaller group sizes so the crew can coordinate tighter on the harder sections.

## So Which Grade Should You Book?

- **First time rafting, traveling with kids, or want a relaxed float?** Start at Grade II.
- **Want a real adventure without going to the extreme?** Grade III is the sweet spot most rafters pick, and it's also our most-booked tier.
- **Experienced, fit, and chasing the biggest rapids on the Ganga?** Grade III+ to IV routes are built for you, and we'll ask a few questions about your prior experience before confirming a booking on these.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can beginners try a Grade III route?**
Yes, with the right expectations. Grade III is accessible to reasonably fit first-timers, unlike Grade IV, which we recommend only for rafters with prior experience or strong water confidence. If you're nervous, Grade II is still the safer starting point.

**Does the grade change with the season?**
Yes. The same stretch can feel noticeably bigger in the September to November post-monsoon peak than in the calmer December to February winter season. See our [best time to raft guide](/blog/best-time-for-rafting-rishikesh) for the season-by-season breakdown.

**Is there a Grade V or VI route in Rishikesh?**
Not commercially. Grade III+ to IV, run on our Kaudiyala routes, is the top of what's offered for guided commercial rafting on this stretch of the Ganga. Higher grades exist on the river system but aren't run as standard packaged trips.
