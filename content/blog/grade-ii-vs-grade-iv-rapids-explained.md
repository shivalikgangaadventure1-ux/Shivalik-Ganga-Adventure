Every rafting package lists a "grade," but what does Grade II or Grade IV actually mean for your trip? Rapid grades follow the International Scale of River Difficulty, a standard used by rafting operators worldwide, running from Grade I (easiest) to Grade VI (essentially unraftable commercially). It measures wave size, obstacle density, how much active paddling and route-reading a rapid demands, and how much margin for error you have if something goes wrong. Here's what each tier we run to Nim Beach actually feels like on the water, not just the textbook definition.

## Grade I-II: Easy to Moderate

Small waves, a few riffles, and mostly clear passages between them. Grade I is close to flat water; Grade II adds gentle, regular waves you can see coming and steer around well in advance. This is where our [Brahmpuri to Nim Beach package](/packages/brahmpuri-to-nim-beach) sits, a gentle, forgiving introduction that's ideal for first-timers, families, and children above the minimum age. Our [Club House to Nim Beach package](/packages/club-house-to-nim-beach) sits just above it, a longer 12 km run with steadier current for rafters who want a little more than the gentlest float. Neither carries real risk of the raft flipping, and both have long calm stretches to relax between rapids.

## Grade III: Moderate to Difficult

Irregular waves, some larger drops, and rapids that require active paddling and teamwork to navigate cleanly. You need to follow your guide's commands promptly, not just paddle when you feel like it. Our two most popular routes, [Shivpuri to Nim Beach](/packages/shivpuri-to-nim-beach) and [Marine Drive to Nim Beach](/packages/marine-drive-to-nim-beach), fall in this range, taking in named rapids like Roller Coaster and Golf Course. Expect to get properly soaked, occasionally lose your footing inside the raft, and need genuine paddling effort through the rapid sequences. Reasonable fitness helps, but you don't need prior rafting experience.

## Grade IV: Difficult to Very Difficult

Powerful, continuous rapids with larger drops, more obstacles, and less margin for error. The gaps between rapids are shorter and the current is stronger throughout. Our longest and most intense run, [Kaudiyala to Nim Beach](/packages/kaudiyala-to-nim-beach), sits here, covering named rapids including Sweet Sixteen and Terminator on top of the Roller Coaster and Golf Course sections further down. This route is best suited to rafters with some prior experience or strong water confidence, runs with senior crew and an extended safety briefing, and uses smaller group sizes so the crew can coordinate tighter on the harder sections.

## So Which Grade Should You Book?

- **First time rafting, traveling with kids, or want a relaxed float?** Start at Grade I-II with Brahmpuri.
- **Want a little more current without a big step up?** Club House is the natural next step.
- **Want a real adventure without going to the extreme?** Grade III is the sweet spot most rafters pick, and Shivpuri and Marine Drive are our most-booked packages.
- **Experienced, fit, and chasing the biggest rapids on the Ganga?** Kaudiyala to Nim Beach, our Grade IV route, is built for you, and we'll ask a few questions about your prior experience before confirming a booking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can beginners try a Grade III route?**
Yes, with the right expectations. Grade III is accessible to reasonably fit first-timers, unlike Grade IV, which we recommend only for rafters with prior experience or strong water confidence. If you're nervous, Grade I-II is still the safer starting point.

**Does the grade change with the season?**
Yes. The same stretch can feel noticeably bigger in the September to November post-monsoon peak than in the calmer December to February winter season. See our [best time to raft guide](/blog/best-time-for-rafting-rishikesh) for the season-by-season breakdown.

**Is there a Grade V or VI route in Rishikesh?**
Not commercially. Grade IV, run on our Kaudiyala to Nim Beach route, is the top of what's offered for guided commercial rafting on this stretch of the Ganga. Higher grades exist on the river system but aren't run as standard packaged trips.
